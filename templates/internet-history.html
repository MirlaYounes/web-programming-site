Timeline of Pre-Web Internet Milestones

1961–1964: Packet Switching Foundations

Leonard Kleinrock published theoretical papers on packet switching at MIT, and Paul Baran at RAND proposed distributed packet networks, establishing the theoretical framework for modern data networking.

Source: Internet Society

October 29, 1969: First ARPANET Transmission

The first message ("LO") was transmitted between host computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute (SRI) on the ARPANET.

Source: Computer History Museum

1973–1974: Invention of TCP/IP

Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn designed the Transmission Control Protocol / Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) to allow disparate computer networks to communicate across a unified architecture.

Source: Internet Society

January 1, 1983: ARPANET Adopts TCP/IP ("Flag Day")

ARPANET officially switched its core network protocol from NCP to TCP/IP, establishing the official operational network baseline for today's Internet.

Source: Internet Society

October 24, 1995: Official Federal Definition of the Internet

The Federal Networking Council (FNC) formally defined the term "Internet" as a global information system logically linked by globally unique IP addresses and communicating via TCP/IP.

Source: Internet Society
