History of the Internet

Key networking infrastructure and protocol developments from 1961 to present.

Introduction

The Internet has developed over many decades from a small research network into a global system used by billions of people. Important developments such as packet switching, TCP/IP, Wi-Fi, mobile networks and IPv6 helped computers and devices communicate more quickly and reliably.

This timeline follows some of the main developments in the history of the Internet from its early foundations in the 1960s to the modern Internet we use today.

Timeline of Internet Milestones

1961–1964: Packet Switching Foundations

Leonard Kleinrock published theoretical papers on packet switching at MIT, and Paul Baran at RAND proposed distributed packet networks, establishing the theoretical framework for modern data networking.

Source: Internet Society

October 29, 1969: First ARPANET Transmission

The first message ("LO") was transmitted between host computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute (SRI) on the ARPANET.

Source: Computer History Museum

1973–1974: Invention of TCP/IP

Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn designed the Transmission Control Protocol / Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) to allow disparate computer networks to communicate across a unified architecture.

Source: Internet Society

January 1, 1983: ARPANET Adopts TCP/IP ("Flag Day")

ARPANET officially switched its core network protocol from NCP to TCP/IP, establishing the official operational network baseline for today's Internet.

Source: Internet Society

October 24, 1995: Official Federal Definition of the Internet

The Federal Networking Council (FNC) formally defined the term "Internet" as a global information system logically linked by globally unique IP addresses and communicating via TCP/IP.

Source: Internet Society

1998: IPv6 Is Standardized

IPv6 was standardized as a new version of the Internet Protocol. It was developed partly because the number of available IPv4 addresses was limited, while the number of devices connecting to the Internet continued to grow.

Source: IETF

Late 1990s–2000s: Growth of Broadband Internet

Broadband connections such as DSL and cable Internet became more common and gradually replaced slower dial-up connections. Faster and always-connected Internet access made downloading files, streaming media and using online services much easier.

1999: Wi-Fi Becomes a Major Wireless Networking Technology

Wi-Fi helped computers and later mobile devices connect to local networks without using physical cables. As wireless routers became common in homes, schools and businesses, Internet access became much more flexible.

2000s: Mobile Internet Expands

Mobile networks increasingly allowed phones and other portable devices to access Internet services. Third-generation mobile systems made faster mobile data practical and helped Internet access move beyond desktop computers.

Source: ITU

2010s: 4G Expands Mobile Broadband

Fourth-generation mobile networks made mobile Internet much faster and supported services such as video streaming, video calls and more advanced mobile applications.

Source: ITU

2020s: Deployment of 5G Networks

5G networks expanded mobile Internet capabilities with higher speeds, lower delays and support for a much larger number of connected devices. This supports modern uses such as smart devices, cloud services and advanced mobile applications.

Source: ITU
